# A scrolling camera built around your handstand

## Core idea

The handstand remains the visual anchor while the page travels through different crops and reveals information around it. The pose stays coherent. A subject mask separates the figure from its rectangular background, so the environment, effects, and typography can change independently.

A useful first motion sequence would be:

| Stage | Camera and mask | Information |
|---|---|---|
| Hero | Full figure; broad light and one flowing gesture around the silhouette | Name, role, and an immediate way to reach work |
| Hands | Push toward the planted hands and forearms; the flowing material opens beside them | Vision and systems work |
| Torso | Pan upward along the same figure; the mask exposes a new layer or palette | Experience and how projects were built |
| Feet | Continue into the legs/feet; a trail or light shape leads into a new composition | Motion and video-editing work |
| Release | Pull back or dissolve the body treatment into a normal document layout | About and contact |

The table is a proposed narrative, not a requirement. The order can change once the art direction is selected.

## What the different drafts suggest

- **01 / Elemental current:** foam and water arcs can carry the camera between body regions.
- **02 / Ink and vapor:** an ink edge can expand from the body contour into a section mask.
- **03 / Solar bloom:** a patch of backlight can reveal the next composition as the camera crosses it.
- **04 / Blackwater:** a silver reflection can become the moving boundary of a project reveal.
- **05 / Risograph:** color impressions can separate and align during the transition.
- **06 / Vision through the body:** source texture and analytical linework can reveal different regions of the figure.
- **07 / Topographic silhouette:** the camera can enter a flowing world contained within the body mask.
- **08 / Luminous afterimage:** trails can resolve into a crisp information layer.
- **09 / Current minimal:** one clear arc can guide a restrained sequence with generous whitespace.
- **10 / Storyboard:** three frames make the full-figure → hands → torso route visible.

## Production approach

For a pan-and-zoom composition, keep the source image and its subject mask aligned in one coordinate system. Move the entire composition's camera transform; keep masks, contour effects, and attached annotations aligned to the same image. Put text on a separate readable layer where appropriate.

Separate the person, a soft trace of the original environment, broad light, and the moving effect into layers. That allows foreground/background parallax and depth without distorting the person.

One source image supports convincing pans, zooms, mask reveals, and layered perspective. A real orbit that reveals the back or unseen sides of the body requires additional views, video, or reconstructed geometry. For this image, the strongest first test is a carefully choreographed layered camera move along the existing silhouette.

Make the scroll sequence bounded and easy to move through. Keep important information reachable in the ordinary page layout. On phones, shorten the camera route and use larger readable crops. A reduced-motion treatment should show useful static compositions.

## Review criteria

The figure should remain recognizable. Hands, feet, and clothing should stay coherent. Attached effects should follow the body rather than slide independently. The environment can transform boldly, while the text remains clear.

The still drafts establish the material and composition. A short motion prototype is the next test once there is a promising combination.
